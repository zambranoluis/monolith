# Monolith — The Work Index

Identity edition 1.0 · 3 October 2026 · Local delivery

## Foundation

**Purpose:** Give work information a clear place and useful relationships.

**Positioning:** A standalone CrimsonTide workspace for pages and structured records, initially serving existing OpenJM individuals. Its OpenJM connection supports reading, explanation, summaries, and planning from selected work information.

**Tagline:** Work, in context.

**Product description:** Monolith brings pages and structured records together in one workspace, so your work has a clear place and OpenJM has useful context to read, explain, summarise, and help you plan.

**Introductory copy:** A place for your pages and records. A clearer starting point for OpenJM.

**Audience:** People who already use OpenJM and need a dependable way to organise the information behind their work. The identity makes room for individual notes, structured detail, and thoughtful review.

**Personality:** Clear, grounded, resourceful, composed. Clear means names and relationships are easy to follow. Grounded means claims stop at the evidence. Resourceful means helping someone find a useful next step. Composed means a steady voice and deliberate hierarchy.

**Brand story:** A note holds an idea. A record holds a detail. Context brings them together. Monolith gives narrative information a page, repeatable information a structure, and connected information a place to be understood. The name suggests a coherent whole made from many pieces; it does not promise an all-in-one replacement for every tool.

## Architecture and naming

Lead with Monolith, capital M, never MONO, Monolith AI, OpenJM Monolith, or a new product name. Uppercase lettering may be used for small functional labels but does not change the proper name. Use “By CrimsonTide” as the secondary endorsement. Spell CrimsonTide as one word with capital C and T. Spell OpenJM exactly; describe the connection separately from the identity lockup.

Use ordinary names for workspace information: workspace, project, page, record, selected source, context, explanation. “The Work Index” names the identity direction; it is not another application or a guaranteed search mechanism. Project names are short, descriptive titles. “Opening brief” is a page; “Project tasks” is a records view.

## Communication

Write straightforward international English. Use organise and summarise. Address the person directly. Prefer a concrete noun to “it” when the source might be unclear. Use one main idea per sentence. Name the next useful action. Keep errors calm, specific, and recoverable. State the source and distinguish recorded information from interpretation.

| Situation | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Interface | Choose pages and records for context. |
| Onboarding | Start with a page for the work you want to organise. Add records when the details need a structure. |
| Empty state | No records yet. Add your first record to keep the details together. |

| Loading example copy | Loading the selected page… |

| Review | This explanation uses the sources you selected. Check what matters before acting. |
| Source limit | This page explains the purpose and priorities; it does not tell you task owners, due dates, or current status. |

Avoid guaranteed, always accurate, automatically synced, understands everything, changes your records, seamless, revolutionary, and claims about security or privacy without an approved policy. Do not present an explanation as permission to act. Do not imply an example is a real live product integration. Mention only the information available in selected sources; a page’s opening date must not appear in a records-only explanation.

## Logo

The symbol uses a 52 × 48 unit frame. Three 12-unit strokes are separated by 8-unit gaps and joined by an 8-unit foundation. Tops are at 8, 20, and 0 units. The centre sits lower so the silhouette is an index gathered into a whole, rather than an ascending chart. The construction stays fixed across every application.

The wordmark is Archivo at weight 700, width 100, with optical spacing. Shipping SVG letters are outlined editable paths, with no font dependency. The endorsement uses Source Sans 3 outlines. Use supplied artwork; do not retype or rearrange it.

- Primary: violet symbol, ink wordmark and endorsement on white or very pale surfaces.
- Monochrome: all ink, for single-ink reproduction.
- Reversed: all white, on ink or violet.
- Wordmark and symbol may be used independently when the product name or context is already clear.
- Clear space: at least 12 symbol units around visible artwork bounds, including the complete endorsed lockup. Scale this unit with the supplied symbol; for the 1.5× lockup symbol it is 18 SVG units.
- Digital minimum width: symbol 16 px, lockup 120 px, endorsed lockup 200 px. Omit the endorsement below its minimum.
- Print minimum width: symbol 5 mm, lockup 30 mm, endorsed lockup 50 mm. Inspect a physical proof for the print process before production.
- Do not stretch, rotate, redraw, outline, rearrange, add gradients or shadows, recolour outside the variants, place on busy imagery, or reduce contrast. Do not use the low-contrast and distorted examples as downloadable artwork.

Body text requires 4.5:1 contrast, large text and meaningful graphics 3:1. Boundaries around controls must also remain recognisable: native checkbox outlines are retained. ## Typography

Archivo provides expressive headings: 700 for the leading phrase and wordmark, 600 for headings. Source Sans 3 provides paragraphs, controls, table labels, and endorsement. Self-host WOFF2 fonts; supply upstream variable TTFs and SIL Open Font License 1.1 notices with redistribution.

The showcase leading phrase scales from 64 to 96 px at desktop and from 60 to 84 px at compact sizes. Section headings range from 38 to 64 px. Body copy is 18 px with 1.5 line-height; long page copy uses 1.7. Utility labels are 12–15 px with concise wording. Keep reading measures around 65–75 characters where space allows. Use tabular numerals for dates and data. Avoid tracking tighter than -0.04em.

## Space, shape, icon, and motion

The spacing foundation is 4 px. Use 8, 12, 16, and 24 px within groups; 32 and 48 px between components; 64 and 96 px between major ideas. The content frame is at most 1280 px wide. Related information aligns to shared rules and baselines. Reflow in reading order on small screens; keep a table’s horizontal scroll inside its labelled region.

Artwork is square and flat. Interface corners use 4 or 8 px radii. App-icon rounded corners belong to the container, not the symbol. No decorative shadows. Use tonal surfaces, rules, and negative space for hierarchy.

Icons use a 24 px viewBox, 1.5 px stroke, round caps and joins, no fills, and concrete functions such as page, records, project, explanation, download, or direction. Decorative icons have hidden accessibility semantics. Essential meaning also has text.

The hero index labels gather in a short sequence: 850 ms, with 0/100/200 ms offsets and exponential ease-out. They are visible throughout. State transitions take 180 ms. Native scrolling remains intact. Reduced motion removes the sequence and smooth scrolling. No loading animation, parallax, auto-scrolling, or continuously moving graphic is required.

## Graphic language and imagery

Use indexed elements, alignment, shared baselines, restrained grids, and visible relationships. White pages and violet index fields form the imagery. For physical concepts, repeat the same symbol and type on a poster and index card. Do not substitute stock office photography, mascots, speculative AI glow, or decorative charts.

Three generated concept boards under assets/concepts informed the direction before implementation. They contain generated lettering and inconsistent exploratory symbol arrangements; keep them labelled as concepts. Production artwork is freshly authored vector geometry and font outlines, never a cropped board with embedded text. The prompt sidecars and assets/PROVENANCE.json document provenance.
